**Annotation Guidelines for Archaeological Entities**

This document provides guidelines for labeling 8 different types of archaeological concepts –characterized as entities– within archaeological texts and excavation reports. These entities are: ARTEFACT, PERIOD, LOCATION, CONTEXT, MATERIAL, SPECIES, PERSON, FEATURE.

**General Guidelines** 
•	Articles are not included in the annotation, e.g., «τέλη 12ου/αρχές 13ου αι.» (of the “late 12th/early 13th c.”) 
•	If the same term is repeated, annotate it every time it appears.
•	Do not annotate general terms without any further specification. E.g., «οικισμός» (“settlement”), «λείψανα» (“remains”), «εύρημα» (“find”), «αρχιτεκτονική κατασκευή» (“architectural construction”), «οικοδόμημα» (“building”).
•	When a descriptive adjective combined with a noun forms a single concept, annotate them as one span. E.g., «διπλός πέλεκυς» (“double axe”), «Ναυάγιο Αντικυθήρων» (“Antikythera Shipwreck”), «αναθηματική στήλη» (votive stele), «βάση του άμβωνα» (ambon base).
•	Do not annotate words indicating quantity, size, or orientation (e.g., πολλά «εργαλεία» – many "tools", μικρά «ναΰδρια» – small "chapels", νότιος «τοίχος» – south "wall"). However, if such words are part of a conventional name, they are annotated as one span. E.g., «νότια βασιλική» (South Basilica).
•	When a term is explained in parentheses, annotate the original term and the explanation separately. E.g., «μαλαχίτη» «(οξείδιο του χαλκού)» ("malachite" "(copper oxide)").

**Entity Definitions** 

**ARTEFACT: any type of archaeological find, regardless of size**
•	Descriptive adjectives that form a single concept together with the artefact noun are included in the same ARTEFACT span (e.g., οικιακό σκεύος – domestic vessel, αναθηματική στήλη – votive stele), except when the adjective denotes material (e.g., «μεταλλικό» εργαλείο – “metal” tool) or time period (e.g., «βυζαντινός» ναός – “Byzantine” church); these belong to the entities MATERIAL and PERIOD respectively.
•	This entity includes architectural members of buildings when they occur as detached, movable individual elements of a structure (e.g., «κιονόκρανο» – capital; τμήματα ἀπὸ τὸν κατεστραμμένο «θριγκὸ» – parts of the destroyed "entablature").
•	Distinguish when a material constitutes an ARTEFACT and when it is the material of an ARTEFACT. For example, bones as finds in a grave belong to ARTEFACT, whereas bones as the material of another object (e.g., «οστέινο» εργαλείο – “bone” tool) are annotated as MATERIAL. 
•	Objects represented in iconographic scenes and reliefs (e.g., jewelry, weapons, furniture) are annotated as ARTEFACT.
•	Literary works, inscriptions, and every type of alphabet are annotated as ARTEFACT, e.g., Ἡ «ἐπιγραφή», ἀποτελούμενη ἀπὸ «συλλαβογράμματα» τῆς «Γραμμικῆς Α γραφῆς» καὶ ἕνα «ἰδεόγραμμα» (The “inscription”, consisting of “syllabograms” of the “Linear A script” and an “ideogram”).

**PERIOD: archaeological periods and dates** 
E.g., «βυζαντινός» ναός, «προϊστορικός» οικισμός του Θορικού (“Byzantine” church; “prehistoric” settlement of Thorikos).
•	Do not annotate modern-era periods and dates.
•	Exclude prefixes like “until" (μέχρι), “about/approximately” (περίπου); annotate only the period. 
•	Annotate surrounding words for multi-year spans. E.g., «από τον 2ο μέχρι το τέλος του 6ου αι. μ.Χ.» (“from the 2nd until the end of the 6th c. AD”), «στη διάρκεια τοῦ 1ου αἰ. π.Χ» (“during the 1st c. BC”). 
•	Annotate entire period names. E.g., «νεότερη νεολιθική περίοδος» (“Late Neolithic period”).
•	Include abbreviations of periods, e.g., «ΠΕ ΙΙΙ» (“EH III” – Early Helladic III), «ΥΕ ΙΙΙΒ/Γ» (“LH IIIB/C” – Late Helladic IIIB/C).
•	Do not annotate indefinite periods, such as «αὐτὴ τὴν περίοδο» (this period).

**LOCATION: cities, villages, locations, countries, rivers, seas, etc.**
•	Do not include very general concepts (e.g., οικισμός – settlement, αιγιαλίτιδα ζώνη – coastal zone). The term settlement is not annotated unless accompanied by a modifier (e.g., Ο ΠΡΟΪΣΤΟΡΙΚΟΣ «ΟΙΚΙΣΜΟΣ ΤΟΥ ΘΟΡΙΚΟΥ” – THE PREHISTORIC «SETTLEMENT OF THORIKOS»).
•	Annotate places such as «Αρχαιολογικό Μουσείο Ιωαννίνων» (“Archaeological Museum of Ioannina”).
•	Directions are not included in the annotation (e.g., βόρειο τμήμα του τεμένους – northern part of the sanctuary), nor are adjectives denoting origin (e.g., αιγύπτιου «ναοφόρου» – of an Egyptian "naophoros").
•	Pay attention to the meaning of the sentence. For example: “The scene of the Ascension of Alexander was particularly popular, from as early as the 10th century, in the Byzantine world and beyond”: here, «Βυζαντινό κόσμο» (Byzantine world) is tagged as LOCATION. 
•	In the phrase: «χάλκινες κοπὲς Κορίνθου» (bronze mints of Corinth), the reference is to Corinthian coinage (ARTΕFACT), not to the city of Corinth. 
•	Villages are annotated together with their province as a single entity (e.g., «Ladochori Igoumenitsas», «Akrotiri Theras»).

**CONTEXT: buildings and anthropogenic constructions that are not finds (ARTΕFACTS), but may contain finds**
•	It includes architectural structures and immovable constructions, e.g., temples, wells, walls, etc. (Detached parts of a monument belong to the category ARTEFACT, e.g., «επιτύμβια στήλη» – grave stele).
•	Do not annotate modern interventions, such as «ανασκαφικές τομές» (excavation trenches).
•	Overly general contexts such as «κτίριο» (building), «μνημείο» (monument), «οικοδόμημα» (edifice), «οικισμός» (settlement) are not tagged, unless accompanied by an adjective or further specification.
•	Conventional names such as «βόρεια βασιλική» (“North Basilica”) are included, but not adjectives denoting orientation, e.g., στον νότιο «τοίχο» (in the south “wall”). 
•	Monuments dedicated to a saint or god are annotated as CONTEXT (e.g., «ναοῦ τοῦ Ἐπικουρίου Ἀπόλλωνος» – “temple of Apollo Epikourios”). The name of the saint/god is not annotated as PERSON.
•	Parts of a citiy (acropolis, agora, cemetery) belong to this category (e.g., they defined the western side of the «city's agora»).

**MATERIAL: Materials from which a find (ARTEFACT) or architectural member (CONTEXT) is made.**
•	Do not annotate adjectives defining materials (e.g., λευκό «μάρμαρο» – white “marble”; «αποκρουσμένο λίθο» – chipped “stone”).
•	Do not annotate very general terms, e.g., αλλόχθονες πρώτες ύλες (allochthonous raw materials).
•	Older architectural members are annotated when reused as building materials (spolia), e.g., «αρχαία αρχιτεκτονικά μέλη» (“ancient architectural members”) used intact or with minimal hewing.
SPECIES: Animal and plant species, e.g., «Αγελάδα» (“Cow”) to the right, suckling her «μοσχαράκι» (“calf”).
•	When a species (e.g., a shell) constitutes the material of an object, it is annotated as MATERIAL; for example, jewelry made of «Spondylus gaederopus».
•	Mythological creatures such as gryphons are included under SPECIES. Mythological beings that speak or act as persons, such as the Sphinx, are annotated as PERSON. 

**PERSON: Historical or mythological figures**
•	Do not annotate individuals living in the modern era.

**FEATURE: stylistic, typological and iconographic characteristics**
•	Do not annotate all descriptive adjectives, only terms that indicate: construction techniques (e.g., «ισοδομικό σύστημα» – “isodomic masonry”; «ψηφιδωτό» – “mosaic”), architectural or painting styles («δικιόνιος σταυροειδής εγγεγραμμένος» ναός – “two-column cross-in-square” church; «ερυθρόμορφο» αγγείο – “red-figure” vase), iconographic themes (e.g., «Ανάληψη του Αλεξάνδρου» – “Ascension of Alexander”; «ένθρονη μορφή» – “enthroned figure”; «Πρόμαχος» – “Promachos”), and artistic trends (e.g., «αιγυπτιάζοντα» αγάλματα – “Egyptianizing” statues).
•	The term «βυζαντινός» (“Byzantine”) is annotated primarily as PERIOD (not FEATURE).
•	Annotate different types of decoration («ανάγλυφη διακόσμηση» – “relief decoration”; «φυτική διακόσμηση» – “floral decoration”; «ενεπίγραφη διακόσμηση» – “inscribed decoration”) without including further details (e.g., «φυτικὴ διακόσμηση» ἀπὸ φύλλο κισσοῦ – “floral decoration” of ivy leaf). Individual iconographic elements of a decoration can be annotated as ARTEFACT (e.g., holding a «τύμπανο» – “drum” with the left hand and a «φιάλη» – “phiale” with the right).
•	This category also includes types of figurines, e.g., «ανθρωπόμορφα και ζωόμορφα» ειδώλια (“anthropomorphic and zoomorphic figurines”), «ιερακόμορφο» ειδώλιο (“falcon-shaped” figurine).

### Completion Criteria
**Requirement:** This record is considered complete only after it has received a minimum of **2** independent annotations.
